PAPER HARDWARE RESULTS
======================

Scope
-----

This file is the paper-facing ledger for the two final hardware scenarios:

  1. Regulation under a 3 g asymmetric aerodynamic payload with no fan.
  2. Mission completion in the forced-start blocked-gap two-fan configuration.

The balanced paper dataset contains:

  3 trials x 2 scenarios x 4 methods = 24 log-supported flights.

The four methods are:

  Mode 0  Nominal TinyMPC
  Mode 1  Analytic in-solver MPC-CBF
  Mode 2  Posthoc LIMO projection with corrected nominal fallback
  Mode 3  Embedded/in-solver LIMO

Supplementary evidence outside the balanced 24-flight table:

  - One additional embedded-LIMO success in Scenario 2.
  - One additional posthoc-LIMO failure in Scenario 2.
  - Two additional nominal Scenario-2 failures reported by the experimenter
    without supplied raw logs.

Do not silently pool those extras into only one arm of the balanced n=3 table.


COMMON HARDWARE AND CONTROLLER CONFIGURATION
============================================

Platform:

  Crazyflie 2.1 Brushless
  Flow Deck v2
  Kalman estimator
  STM32F405 at 168 MHz
  Out-of-tree controller selected as controller 6

Common outer-loop configuration recorded in the flight logs:

  MPC/model rate:                   20 Hz / 20 Hz
  Control deadline:                 50 ms = 8,400,000 CPU cycles
  ADMM iterations:                  5
  rho:                              5
  Qz:                               100
  R:                                4
  MPC-to-PID bridge:                prediction index x10
  Common Z guard:                   [reference, reference + 0.20 m]
  X/Y bridge clamp:                 none
  Figure-8 source:                  traj_fig8_12.h
  Figure-8 source rate:             100 Hz
  Figure-8 duration:                approximately 24.07 s
  Figure-8 repetitions:             two laps
  Console sampling:                 every second 20 Hz MPC step

The stock inner PID consumes the common MPC position bridge in every arm.
Consequently, the paper compares four outer-loop MPC/safety constructions
under the same inner-loop stabilization, not four direct motor controllers.

Metric definitions:

  Lateral tracking error:
    sqrt((x - x_ref)^2 + (y - y_ref)^2)

  Z tracking error:
    z - z_ref

  Tilt magnitude:
    sqrt(roll^2 + pitch^2)

  RMSE:
    root mean square over the stated analysis window.

  Exact maximum step:
    cumulative maximum of every raw DWT-timed control step, including steps
    omitted by console decimation.

  Deadline overrun:
    complete control step greater than 8,400,000 DWT cycles.

Integrity rules applied to both scenarios:

  - Metrics are computed per flight, then flight-level metrics are averaged.
  - Samples after supervisor lock or the first physical-failure estimator
    reset are invalid and excluded.
  - A post-lock TRAJ DONE or LANDING COMPLETE print is not accepted as mission
    completion.
  - Failed flights with different valid durations are not pooled into the
    completed-flight tracking table.


SCENARIO 1: 3 G ASYMMETRIC AERODYNAMIC PAYLOAD, NO FAN
=======================================================

Protocol
--------

Payload:

  3 g of paper attached to the front-right propeller guard for every flight.

Disturbance introduced by the attachment:

  - added mass;
  - off-center center-of-mass shift;
  - inertia mismatch;
  - orientation- and velocity-dependent aerodynamic drag and torque;
  - local propeller/guard inflow interference;
  - possible flexible flutter.

No payload-specific mass, center-of-mass, inertia, aerodynamic, motor, model,
controller, or gain compensation was applied.

Other conditions:

  External fan:                    none
  Target/reference altitude:      0.70 m
  Takeoff rule:                   gated climb and post-takeoff settling
  Trajectory origin:              post-takeoff settled position
  Primary analysis window:        trajectory t >= 3 s through TRAJ DONE
  Trials per method:              3
  Total flights:                  12
  Valid trajectory completions:  12/12
  Controlled landing evidence:   present in all except MPC-CBF run 3, whose
                                 capture ends after landing was requested
  Deadline overruns:             0 across all 12 flights

Learned-component attribution
-----------------------------

MPC-CBF is near-nominal on every reported tracking and attitude axis:

  - lateral RMSE differs by 0.6%;
  - Z RMSE differs by 4.2%;
  - tilt RMSE differs by 0.03%;
  - the corresponding peak metrics differ by at most 1.2%.

This is not an inert analytic baseline. Its 20-stage constraint was installed
on every solve and 99.4% of installed row projections intersected. Aligning
the analytic boundary to the 0.70 m reference steel-mans the baseline: the
vertical half-space is continuously present, but it does not create the
attitude protection or learned authority trade observed with embedded LIMO.

Relative to this active analytic baseline, embedded LIMO reduces Z RMSE by
42.6% and tilt RMSE by 32.1%.

Reproducibility
---------------

The original single-triplet pilot reproduced the final qualitative ordering.
Embedded Z RMSE changed by only 1.1%, from 0.00889 m to 0.00879 m. Across all
listed nominal, embedded, and posthoc metrics, the final n=3 means remain
within approximately 7% of the corresponding first-flight values.

The defensible claim is reproducible ordering with especially close vertical
agreement. Do not claim approximately 1% agreement for every metric.

Timing-maximum audit
--------------------

The identical displayed 47.701 ms embedded and posthoc maxima are a
three-decimal rounding coincidence:

  Embedded run 2: 8,013,755 cycles = 47.700923 ms
  Posthoc run 3:  8,013,731 cycles = 47.700780 ms

The raw values differ by 24 DWT cycles, came from different flights and
trajectory steps, and were recorded by an unclamped cumulative maximum.

Scenario-1 paper conclusion
---------------------------

Under a fixed asymmetric payload with no fan, all four arms complete the
mission. Embedded LIMO trades 18-23% higher lateral tracking RMSE for the best
vertical and attitude regulation: 42.6% lower Z RMSE and 32.1% lower tilt RMSE
than an active, aligned in-solver analytic MPC-CBF baseline. Posthoc projection
is infeasible on 61.6% of attempts and has the worst vertical regulation.
The learned arm adds compute but records zero deadline overruns.


SCENARIO 2: FORCED-START BLOCKED-GAP TWO-FAN MISSION
====================================================

Protocol
--------

Trajectory:

  Two-lap Figure-8 from traj_fig8_12.h.

Start rule:

  The Figure-8 begins exactly 2.0 s after OOT takeoff begins. There is no
  altitude, settling, or vertical-velocity requirement. X, Y, and Z reference
  origin are measured at the forced timed start. The Z error window begins at
  trajectory t + 3 s.

Fan/obstacle geometry recorded by the experimenter:

  - one upward-blowing fan perpendicular to the vehicle;
  - one downward-blowing fan directly in front of the vehicle;
  - the downward fan rests on a box;
  - another box blocks the gap between the two fans.

The exact fan speed, calibrated wind field, distance, battery randomization,
and trial order are not encoded in the saved logs. The saved blocked-gap notes
also do not establish that the 3 g paper payload remained attached. Therefore,
this scenario must be described as the fixed blocked-gap two-fan mission, not
silently relabeled as a calibrated wind or 3 g payload trial.

Primary endpoint:

  Complete all 482 trajectory steps and reach controlled landing without a
  supervisor lock, estimator-reset crash, or physical fan collision.

Balanced paper subset:

  Embedded:  runs 1-3 of four saved runs
  Posthoc:   runs 1-3 of four saved runs
  MPC-CBF:   all three saved runs
  Nominal:   the three supplied raw logs, named runs 3-5

The subset contains 12 log-supported flights. It was balanced after collection,
not preregistered. This does not change the observed ordering: the excluded
embedded run also succeeds, the excluded posthoc run also fails, and both
additional nominal attempts were reported as failures.

Scenario-2 mechanism and timing conclusion
------------------------------------------

The mission ordering is not explained by an observed compute overload:

  - every flight with valid timed trajectory samples recorded zero 50 ms
    deadline overruns; nominal takeoff-lock run 4 has no valid tracking timing;
  - embedded completed all three flights with a worst exact step of 44.323 ms;
  - balanced posthoc failed despite a worst exact step of 40.881 ms;
    supplemental posthoc run 4 also failed with a 42.953 ms maximum;
  - MPC-CBF failed despite a worst exact step of 31.548 ms;
  - logged nominal failure runs remained below 30.399 ms.

Embedded learned rows were active and binding throughout every retained
completed-flight primary window. Applied authority was 1.0 and applied Qz
scale was 1.4. This is a saturated stress-regime test, not an authority-sweep
or benign-tax experiment.

Posthoc infeasibility rises from 61.6% in Scenario 1 to 89.2% before failure in
Scenario 2. The analytic MPC-CBF constraints are active and binding yet do not
prevent failure. Embedded LIMO is the only method that completes the fixed
mission.

Scenario-2 paper conclusion
---------------------------

In the fixed forced-start blocked-gap two-fan mission, embedded LIMO completes
3/3 balanced trials, while nominal, posthoc, and analytic MPC-CBF each complete
0/3. A fourth embedded flight also completes, a fourth posthoc flight also
fails, and two additional nominal failures were reported. The result is a
mission-level success claim for this specific configuration. Because several
baseline failures involve physical fan or obstacle contact, do not present it
as a clean aerodynamic-force isolation study.


CROSS-SCENARIO SYNTHESIS
========================

The two scenarios support complementary claims.

1. Continuous regulation under model mismatch

   In Scenario 1, every method completes. Embedded LIMO provides substantially
   better vertical and attitude regulation than nominal, posthoc, and an active
   analytic MPC-CBF, at the cost of higher lateral tracking error.

2. Learned-component attribution

   Analytic MPC-CBF is active on 99.4% of installed row projections in
   Scenario 1 but remains near nominal on all measured axes. Embedded LIMO
   improves Z RMSE by 42.6% and tilt RMSE by 32.1% relative to that analytic
   in-solver baseline. Thus in-solver placement alone does not explain the
   result; the learned barrier/authority construction does.

3. Mission completion under severe combined disturbance and geometry

   In Scenario 2, embedded completes 3/3 balanced flights while each baseline
   completes 0/3. This turns the regulation result into a mission-level result.

4. Posthoc mechanism

   Corrected posthoc fallback is a no-op when infeasible. Posthoc is infeasible
   on 61.6% of Scenario-1 attempts and 89.2% of valid balanced Scenario-2
   attempts.
   Scenario-1 vertical degradation is therefore caused by feasible
   action-changing interventions, while Scenario 2 additionally demonstrates
   the limited availability of the one-step projection under severe demand.

5. Embedded cost

   Embedded LIMO has the highest or near-highest computation, but all flights
   record zero deadline overruns. In Scenario 1 the barrier MLP/gradient, not
   the 11-parameter authority policy, dominates learned-stage latency.

Paper claims directly supported:

  - Embedded learned safety runs on an STM32F405 inside a 20 Hz MPC loop.
  - Zero control-deadline overruns occurred in both final scenarios.
  - Embedded LIMO improves vertical and attitude regulation under a fixed
    asymmetric 3 g payload.
  - The improvement is not reproduced by a continuously active analytic
    in-solver MPC-CBF.
  - Embedded LIMO is the only method to complete the balanced blocked-gap
    two-fan mission.
  - Posthoc learned projection is frequently infeasible on target.

Claims not supported by these data:

  - universal superiority across all disturbances or trajectories;
  - improved lateral tracking in Scenario 1;
  - a pure aerodynamic explanation for Scenario-2 failures;
  - calibrated wind-force or fan-speed dose response in Scenario 2;
  - randomized or blinded trial ordering;
  - authority-schedule adaptation in these two 0.70 m stress scenarios;
  - statistical population-level failure probabilities from n=3.

Recommended paper framing:

  The no-fan payload table is the clean continuous-metric attribution result.
  The blocked-gap two-fan table is the mission-completion result. Present them
  together: the first shows what the learned component changes when all methods
  finish, and the second shows why that change matters when the task becomes
  failure-limited.

FINAL PAPER TAKEAWAY
====================

The final hardware evidence is not a single cherry-picked win. It is a
two-part result across 24 balanced, log-supported flights:

  - When all methods finish under an asymmetric 3 g payload, embedded LIMO
    gives the best vertical and attitude regulation and decisively outperforms
    an active analytic MPC-CBF on those axes.

  - When the fixed mission becomes failure-limited, embedded LIMO completes
    3/3 flights while nominal, posthoc, and MPC-CBF each complete 0/3.

The learned method pays a measurable compute and lateral-tracking cost, but it
stays inside the MCU deadline. That honest tradeoff, the analytic-baseline
attribution, the posthoc infeasibility mechanism, and the mission-completion
gap are the complete hardware story.
