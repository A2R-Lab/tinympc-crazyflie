HOVER UNDER WIND LEVEL 3: EMBEDDED LIMO VS LIMO POSTHOC
=======================================================

Window and boundary
-------------------

Metrics use the first EXP sample at/after OOT+3.0 s through the final EXP
sample before TRAJ DONE. PID landing is excluded.

At the first sample, boundary-relative error is defined as zero:

  delta_e_xy(t) = e_xy(t) - e_xy(OOT+3-s boundary)

The user-confirmed primary protocol refreshes every performance channel at
this same boundary:

  delta_e_z(t)    = e_z(t) - e_z(OOT+3-s boundary)
  delta_roll(t)   = roll(t) - roll(OOT+3-s boundary)
  delta_pitch(t)  = pitch(t) - pitch(OOT+3-s boundary)

Boundary RMSE therefore measures additional lateral error accumulated after
the fixed wind boundary. The primary Z and tilt metrics measure error and
attitude change from that same boundary. Conventional absolute lateral/Z
tracking and absolute tilt are retained in the original pair tables below
as secondary audit metrics.

Cross-task mechanism statement
------------------------------

With a constant hover reference, there is almost no lateral tracking
performance to give up, so backing off the lateral weight costs almost
nothing and the reallocated authority appears as improved disturbance
rejection. On an aggressive trajectory there is real tracking to sacrifice,
so the same backoff has a visible lateral-tracking price. The trade costs
performance only when there is a task to sacrifice.

The hover table therefore measures the benign stationary-reference regime.
It should be read together with the Figure-8 Pareto result, not as a claim
that embedded LIMO improves every metric in every maneuver.

The approximately 0.46 m refreshed Z RMSE is not a tracking-failure metric:
each arm begins OOT near z=0.55 m with a 1.00 m reference, then removes that
initial approximately -0.45 m Z error by climbing. Subtracting the initial Z
error therefore turns successful altitude acquisition into an approximately
+0.45 m error change for most of the flight. This is why OOT-start-relative
Z and tilt dilute the disturbance comparison and remain secondary.

Posthoc made its whole-flight projection attempts during this initial climb:
58/65 were infeasible (89.2%). The fixed OOT+3 primary window excludes the
shared altitude-acquisition transient and remains the paper comparison.

Learned-authority sim-to-real check
-----------------------------------

The canonical 1.00 m hardware-emulation simulation reports mean embedded-LIMO
authority of 0.1333 with no wind and 0.2224 in its light-wind band:

  prereg_audit/quad_hover_hardware_eval/results.md

Across the 450 embedded-LIMO primary-window hardware samples, the applied
authority occupied exactly three adjacent entries of the deployed 16-level
cache bank:

Applied authority   Index   Samples   Fraction
------------------  ------  --------  --------
0.1333              2       317       70.44%
0.2000              3       131       29.11%
0.2667              4         2        0.44%

The pooled hardware mean was 0.1531 applied and 0.1606 requested. Therefore
the no-wind simulation mean coincides exactly with the dominant hardware
cache level, while the simulated light-wind mean is bracketed by the next two
hardware levels. This is evidence that the frozen learned scheduler operates
on hardware in the authority regime predicted by simulation after float32
porting, 16-level quantization, and static-bank installation.

Do not claim that the hardware light-wind mean matches 0.2224 to three digits:
the physical fan level is not calibrated to the simulation wind band, and the
measured hardware mean is lower. A paper figure should overlay the hardware
authority trace or occupancy histogram with horizontal simulation references
at 0.1333 and 0.2224.
