Boundary-relative error subtracts the XY tracking-error vector at the start
of the primary window:

  e_xy(t)       = position_xy(t) - reference_xy(t)
  delta_e_xy(t) = e_xy(t) - e_xy(first sample at/after OOT + 3 s)

User-confirmed primary reporting refreshes every performance channel at this
same boundary:

  delta_e_z(t)    = e_z(t) - e_z(boundary)
  delta_roll(t)   = roll(t) - roll(boundary)
  delta_pitch(t)  = pitch(t) - pitch(boundary)

Accordingly, the primary Z and tilt quantities below measure error/attitude
change after the wind-analysis boundary. Absolute XY/Z tracking and absolute
tilt are retained later in this file as secondary audit metrics.

Cross-task mechanism statement
------------------------------

With a constant hover reference, there is almost no lateral tracking
performance to give up, so backing off the lateral weight costs almost
nothing and the reallocated authority appears as improved disturbance
rejection. On an aggressive trajectory there is real tracking to sacrifice,
so the same backoff has a visible lateral-tracking price. The trade costs
performance only when there is a task to sacrifice.

This Figure-8 table is therefore a safety--tracking Pareto result. At w=1.0,
the deployed cache reduces XY position weight from 100 to 10, raises Z
position weight from 100 to 140, and raises attitude weights from 4 to 6.
Report the lateral tracking cost beside the Z and tilt benefits.

Posthoc tracks the deliberately aggressive lateral reference more closely,
but embedded LIMO substantially improves altitude regulation and attitude
while costing essentially the same on-target time. The 73.0% posthoc

posthoc run displayed larger primary-window Z and attitude excursions
alongside frequent infeasibility.

This one pair supports a placement trade-off, not a population-level claim.
Repeat matched pairs and a nominal wind arm are required before reporting an
aggregate method comparison.

Wind versus no-wind nominal audit
---------------------------------

The calm-air nominal run in figure8_070_no_wind_three_way_comparison.txt uses
the same trajectory and OOT+3-s-to-end window. Its boundary-relative XY RMSE
was 0.6979 m and its conventional lateral tracking RMSE was 0.4666 m.

Metric                              No wind      Downward-wind mean   Change
Mean XY boundary error, m           0.6042       0.5757               -4.7%
XY boundary/error-change RMSE, m    0.6979       0.6795               -2.6%
Peak XY boundary error, m           1.2235       1.2701               +3.8%
Conventional lateral RMSE, m        0.4666       0.4682               +0.3%
Z error-change RMSE, m              0.0177       0.0191               +8.2%
Tilt-change RMSE, deg               13.912       14.749               +6.0%

Thus the downward-wind campaign did not meaningfully worsen average lateral
tracking: conventional lateral RMSE is only 0.3% higher, while the
boundary-refreshed XY RMSE is 2.6% lower. The wind effect is clearer in peak
XY error (+3.8%), Z error-change (+8.2%), and tilt change (+6.0%). Treat the
small average-XY differences as trial variability, not a wind benefit.
