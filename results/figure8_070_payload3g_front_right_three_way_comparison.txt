FIGURE-EIGHT AT 0.70 M WITH 3 G FRONT-RIGHT PAYLOAD: RUN 1
================================================================

Status
------

Single matched three-way payload triplet:

  - nominal TinyMPC (mode 0)
  - embedded LIMO (mode 3)
  - posthoc LIMO (mode 2)

All three flights completed controller-6 takeoff, both Figure-8 laps, and
controlled landing without a deadline overrun or crash.

Experimental condition
----------------------

Test identity:
  This is the earlier matched nominal-versus-embedded-LIMO-versus-posthoc-LIMO
  experiment in which a physical 3 g paper payload was attached to the
  vehicle. "Aerodynamic payload test" in these notes always means this
  attached-paper experiment. It does not mean a no-payload fan test, and it
  must not be confused with later post-crash hover sanity checks.

Payload:
  3 g of paper attached to the front-right propeller guard.

Attachment consistency:
  The same 3 g paper placement was used for all three arms in this matched
  triplet. The payload remained physically attached throughout each flight.

Interpretation:
  This is an asymmetric payload/center-of-mass and aerodynamic perturbation,
  not merely a centered added-mass test.

How the payload breaks the initial model:

  No payload-specific mass, center-of-mass, inertia, aerodynamic, motor, or
  propeller-flow correction was introduced into the MPC dynamics, reference
  generator, learned barrier, or frozen authority policy. Relative to the
  model used to construct the controller, the attached paper therefore adds:

    1. Added mass, changing hover thrust and translational acceleration.
    2. An off-center center of mass, producing a static roll/pitch moment and
       asymmetric motor loading.
    3. A changed inertia tensor and principal axes, perturbing the modeled
       attitude and angular-rate dynamics.
    4. Orientation- and velocity-dependent aerodynamic force and torque: the
       paper behaves as a small sail even in nominally calm air because the
       vehicle moves rapidly through the Figure-8.
    5. Local propeller/guard inflow interference at the front-right rotor.
    6. Possible flexible-body flutter, making the disturbance time varying
       rather than a single fixed parameter offset.

  This is therefore a coupled structured sim-to-real plant mismatch, designed
  to violate the initial rigid-body/nominal-aerodynamics model rather than to
  isolate added mass alone. The barrier and authority-policy weights remained
  frozen. These data support robustness to the combined mismatch; they do not
  identify which individual mechanism caused each observed error.

Wind:
  No wind was reported for this triplet; treat it as a calm-air payload test.
  If an external fan is added in a later payload trial, record its level,
  direction, distance, onset, and attachment orientation separately. The paper
  couples strongly to airflow, so such a trial is a payload-plus-aerodynamic-
  disturbance experiment and must not be pooled with this calm-air triplet.

  Trajectory:               traj_fig8_12.h
  Laps:                     2
  Trajectory duration:      approximately 24.1 s
  MPC/model rate:           20 Hz

Primary analysis window
-----------------------

Use:

  TRAJ START + 3 s through TRAJ DONE

Historical notes may call this "OOT + 3 s," but that name is no longer exact
for this gated-takeoff firmware. Controller 6 is activated on the ground about
9 s before the Figure-8 starts. The firmware resets the EXP timestamp at
TRAJ START, so the primary filter is:

  EXP tms >= 3000

Landing is excluded. The first retained console samples occur at approximately
3.08 s, and each arm contributes 211 decimated samples.

Metric definitions:

  Lateral RMSE = sqrt(mean((x - x_ref)^2 + (y - y_ref)^2))
  Peak lateral error = max(sqrt((x - x_ref)^2 + (y - y_ref)^2))
  Z RMSE = sqrt(mean((z - z_ref)^2))
  Peak Z error = max(|z - z_ref|)
  Tilt = sqrt(roll^2 + pitch^2), in degrees

Relative effects
----------------

Embedded LIMO relative to nominal:

  Z RMSE improvement:          45.9%
  Peak Z error improvement:    40.5%
  Tilt RMSE improvement:       32.9%
  Peak tilt improvement:       22.6%
  Lateral RMSE degradation:    26.1%
  Peak lateral degradation:    13.3%

Embedded LIMO relative to posthoc:

  Z RMSE improvement:          76.9%
  Peak Z error improvement:    74.0%
  Tilt RMSE improvement:       28.6%
  Peak tilt improvement:       14.7%
  Lateral RMSE degradation:    17.9%
  Peak lateral degradation:    10.7%

Interpretation:

  Nominal produces the best lateral tracking on the aggressive Figure-8.
  Embedded LIMO gives up lateral tracking authority but provides substantially
  tighter vertical regulation and lower attitude excursion under the
  asymmetric payload. Posthoc lies between the two laterally, but has the
  worst vertical regulation.

switching is consistent with its larger Z error and tilt.

statistical comparison.
