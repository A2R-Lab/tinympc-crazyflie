FIGURE-EIGHT AT 0.70 M, NO WIND: THREE-WAY COMPARISON
======================================================

Status
------

Calm-air diagnostic triplet (run 1): nominal MPC, embedded LIMO, and posthoc
LIMO. These flights deliberately operate below the 0.75 m high-altitude skip
to exercise the learned safety path. They must not be pooled with the earlier
1.00 m figure-eight wind pair.

Aggressive trajectory characterization
---------------------------------------

The table-driven reference was generated at 100 Hz and is resampled onto the
20 Hz embedded MPC grid. Resampling is correct, but it does not reduce the
physical speed of the trajectory.

  X span:                           1.005 m
  Y span:                           2.384 m

  Peak commanded speed:             2.457 m/s
  Peak reference tilt:              17.609 deg

Measured peak vehicle speed in the primary window:

  Nominal:       1.365 m/s

None of the three arms reproduced the reference's 2.457 m/s peak speed.
Nominal reached only 55.6% of the commanded peak; embedded reached 40.8%.
The large lateral errors therefore reflect an intentionally aggressive
closed-loop stress condition, not only the choice of safety method.

Posthoc mechanism
-----------------

Whole-flight firmware summary:

  Projection attempts: 366
  Infeasible:           239
  Infeasibility rate:   65.3%

In the decimated primary-window records:

  Feasible projection samples:   60
  Infeasible/fallback samples:    96
  Projection-inactive samples:    55
  Samples with actual z > 0.75 m: 54
  Inactive among z > 0.75 m:      54/54

The observed vertical switching is consistent with the posthoc mechanism:
a feasible projection can raise commanded Z as high as 0.896 m; crossing
0.75 m triggers the high-altitude skip and returns the command toward 0.70 m.
Infeasible projections independently switch to the corrected nominal
fallback. Embedded LIMO instead held an essentially constant Z command.

Interpretation
--------------

This single calm-air triplet supports a placement trade-off, not a universal
tracking win. Nominal MPC provides the best lateral tracking and lowest
compute cost. Embedded LIMO substantially improves vertical regulation and
attitude, at the cost of lateral tracking and approximately 12.3 ms of median
compute. Posthoc retains near-nominal lateral RMSE but its vertical
instability coincides with frequent infeasibility, feasible upward
projections, nominal fallback, and high-altitude skip transitions.

The 0.70 m embedded arm is saturated in calm air, so this is an always-active
barrier stress condition rather than the calibrated adaptive regime. Repeat
matched triplets are required before reporting aggregate paper results.

Paper framing
-------------

Defensible description:

  "To probe the limits of the 20 Hz embedded closed-loop stack, we retain the
  unscaled simulator-generated figure-eight reference as an aggressive
  tracking stress test. The trajectory completes each lap in 12.04 s, with
  median and peak commanded speeds of 1.52 and 2.46 m/s, respectively. All
  controller variants receive the identical reference and hardware
  configuration."

This result should be labeled a secondary high-speed robustness stress test,
not the primary nominal tracking benchmark. A slower or spatially scaled
figure-eight should be used separately for the primary accuracy-under-wind
comparison. Because the stress-test classification was made after
characterizing the commanded speed, the paper should say the trajectory was
"retained and classified as a stress test" rather than imply that the metric
was pre-specified before observing the flights.
